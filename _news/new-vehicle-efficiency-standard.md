A revised vehicle efficiency standard is set to lower the average emissions target manufacturers must hit across their combined new-vehicle fleet, with penalties for exceeding the target scaling with how far a fleet's average sits above the line.

## Why utes and large wagons are more exposed

Because the standard applies to a manufacturer's whole fleet average, heavier vehicles like dual-cab utes and large wagons have less room to move before pulling a manufacturer's average above target — which is a large part of why hybrid and more efficient variants of these vehicles are being prioritised industry-wide.

## What this could mean for pricing

Manufacturers facing penalties for missing efficiency targets may pass some of that cost on through pricing of their least efficient variants, while more efficient variants (hybrids, smaller-capacity turbo engines) could see comparatively smaller price increases or additional incentives.

## What buyers should watch

Buyers planning to purchase a new ute or large wagon in the next 12–18 months should watch for manufacturers announcing updated engine lineups or hybrid variants aimed specifically at meeting the tightened target.
