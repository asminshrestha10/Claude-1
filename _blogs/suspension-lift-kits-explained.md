"Lift kit" gets used loosely, but body lifts and suspension lifts do fundamentally different things — and only one of them meaningfully improves off-road ability.

## Body lifts

A body lift inserts spacers between the vehicle's body and its chassis, raising the body (and therefore ground clearance under the bodywork) without touching the suspension. It's a cheaper way to fit larger tyres, but it does nothing for wheel travel, approach/departure angles at the bumpers, or suspension geometry.

## Suspension lifts

A suspension lift replaces or extends the springs, shocks and sometimes control arms, physically raising the vehicle at the axles. This genuinely increases ground clearance at every point of the vehicle, and — done properly — can improve wheel articulation.

## Getting the geometry right

A poorly executed suspension lift can introduce bad driveline angles, reduced articulation from over-extended shocks, or bump-steer from incorrectly geometried control arms. A quality kit accounts for these factors; a cheap "spring spacer only" lift often doesn't.

## How much lift do you actually need?

For most touring and moderate off-road use, a 40–50mm lift is enough to fit a meaningfully larger all-terrain tyre without dramatically changing the vehicle's on-road manners or requiring extensive driveline modification.
