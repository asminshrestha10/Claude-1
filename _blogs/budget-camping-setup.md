It's easy to look at a fully kitted-out touring rig and assume that's the entry price for weekend camping. It isn't. Most experienced tourers built their setup gradually, and there's a sensible order to do it in.

## Start with recovery, not comfort

Before you spend on camping comfort, make sure you can get yourself unstuck. A basic recovery kit — rated shackles, a snatch strap, and a set of traction boards — costs far less than a single rooftop tent and matters more the first time you need it.

## Sleeping: swag before rooftop tent

A quality canvas swag is a fraction of the price of a rooftop tent and works from any vehicle, no roof rack required. It's a sensible first step before committing to roof-mounted gear.

## Cooking and power

A basic gas cooker and an esky will get you through your first several trips. A dual battery system and a compressor fridge are worthwhile upgrades once you're doing regular multi-day trips and want to stop buying ice.

## A realistic build order

1. Recovery kit
2. Swag or basic tent
3. Cooking setup (gas stove, basic cookware)
4. Water storage
5. Dual battery + fridge (once trip frequency justifies it)
6. Roof rack + rooftop tent (optional, later upgrade)
