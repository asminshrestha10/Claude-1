Using the wrong recovery point during a snatch or winch recovery is one of the most dangerous mistakes in off-road driving. A failed recovery point under tension can turn into a projectile.

## What makes a point "rated"

A proper recovery point is bolted directly to the vehicle's chassis rail (not just the bodywork) and rated to a specific working load in kilonewtons. Factory tie-down points and tow balls are usually **not** rated for dynamic recovery loads.

## Front vs rear recovery points

Most 4x4s benefit from having rated points at both ends, since you can't always predict which direction a recovery will come from. Aftermarket bull bars and rear bars often include factory-rated recovery points as part of the bar itself.

## A simple pre-trip checklist

1. Confirm your recovery points are chassis-mounted, not just bolted to a plastic bumper.
2. Check the rated capacity against your vehicle's GVM plus a safety margin.
3. Inspect mounting bolts for corrosion or looseness before every trip.
4. Carry a rated bow shackle or soft shackle matched to your recovery point.
