Airing down increases your tyre's contact patch with the ground, which improves traction and comfort over uneven or soft terrain. The right pressure depends heavily on what you're driving over.

## General starting points

These are starting points, not rules — always adjust based on load, tyre size and how the vehicle is behaving.

- **Formed gravel tracks:** 28–32 psi
- **Corrugated dirt roads:** 22–26 psi
- **Sand:** 15–18 psi
- **Rocky, technical terrain:** 16–20 psi

## Why sand needs the lowest pressure

Sand offers almost no lateral support, so a wider, flatter contact patch is critical to avoid digging in. Going too low, though, increases the risk of the tyre rolling off the rim under hard cornering — stay above the point where the sidewall starts to visibly bulge under load.

## Don't forget to re-inflate

Driving any distance on sealed roads at off-road pressures overheats and damages tyres. Always carry a 12V compressor and re-inflate before returning to the highway.

## A note on tyre pressure monitoring

A tyre pressure monitoring system takes the guesswork out of airing down consistently across all four (or six) tyres, and warns you immediately if a puncture starts leaking pressure on the trail.
