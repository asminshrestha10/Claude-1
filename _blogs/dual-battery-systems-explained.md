A dual battery system lets you run a fridge, lighting or other accessories from a second ("auxiliary") battery without draining the battery your engine needs to start.

## Why simple isolators aren't enough anymore

Older dual battery setups used a voltage-sensing relay to connect the two batteries whenever the alternator was charging. Modern vehicles with smart alternators (which vary output voltage to save fuel) often don't charge consistently enough for a simple isolator to fully charge an auxiliary battery — which is where a DC-DC charger comes in.

## What a DC-DC charger actually does

A DC-DC charger takes the variable voltage from the vehicle's alternator and converts it into the correct multi-stage charging profile (bulk, absorption, float) for the auxiliary battery, regardless of what the smart alternator is doing. Many units also include solar input, letting you top up from a portable panel using the same charging profile.

## Sizing it correctly

The charger's output current needs to match your auxiliary battery's capacity and how much driving time you realistically have between uses — undersizing the charger means a battery that never quite reaches full charge on shorter trips.
