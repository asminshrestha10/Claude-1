The Simpson Desert crossing is one of the more accessible serious desert trips available to properly prepared vehicles, but "accessible" still means over a thousand dune crossings and long stretches without fuel or phone signal.

## Fuel and water planning

We ran two long-range tanks plus jerry cans, aiming for a comfortable margin over the calculated distance rather than the bare minimum. Water was budgeted well above standard recommendations, since a breakdown in the middle of the crossing can mean days of delay, not hours.

## Dune driving technique

Momentum matters more than power on the steep faces of Simpson dunes — backing off too early on the approach is the most common reason vehicles get stuck cresting a dune. Airing down before entering the dune field made a noticeable difference to how easily the vehicle floated over soft sand rather than digging in.

## What we'd change next time

- Carry a taller sand flag earlier in the trip — dune crest visibility is a genuine safety issue with oncoming traffic.
- Pack fewer "just in case" tools and more of the specific spares we actually needed (fan belt, radiator hose).
- Air down slightly earlier than we did; the first hour of dunes was harder than it needed to be.

A desert crossing like this rewards preparation over bravado — the vehicles that struggled on our trip were generally under-prepared for fuel and recovery, not under-powered.
