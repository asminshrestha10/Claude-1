A winch's advertised rating — say, 4,500kg — is measured under specific conditions: a single layer of cable on the drum, and a straight pull. Real-world recoveries rarely match that exactly.

## Line pull drops as the drum fills

A winch's pulling force is highest with a single wrap of cable on the drum and decreases with each additional layer, because the effective drum diameter increases and torque is fixed. By the time you're on the outer layers, pulling force can be noticeably lower than the rated figure.

## Snatch blocks and mechanical advantage

Running the winch cable through a snatch block (pulley) attached to a separate anchor point and back to the vehicle doubles the mechanical advantage, roughly halving the load on the winch motor — at the cost of halving line speed. This is the standard way to recover a genuinely stuck vehicle without exceeding a winch's capability.

## Why straight-line pulls matter

Winching at an angle instead of straight ahead causes the cable to pile up unevenly on one side of the drum, which can damage the winch and reduce effective pulling capacity. Where possible, reposition the anchor point or use a pulley to keep the pull as straight as practical.

## A basic safety rule

Never straddle or stand near a winch cable under tension. If a cable or recovery point fails, the stored energy releases suddenly and violently — always keep bystanders clear and use a dampener (like a heavy blanket) over the line as standard practice.
