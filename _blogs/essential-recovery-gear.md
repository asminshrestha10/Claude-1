There's no single "correct" recovery kit — what you carry should match the terrain you actually drive. This is a sensible baseline for remote touring in Australia.

## The core kit

- Rated recovery points (front and rear)
- A snatch strap or kinetic rope, rated to your vehicle's weight
- Rated bow shackles or soft shackles
- A set of traction boards
- A basic first-aid kit and a way to call for help outside phone range (satellite messenger or UHF/HF radio)

## Why traction boards go a long way

Traction boards recover a huge proportion of "bogged in sand or mud" situations without needing a second vehicle or a winch at all — they're often the highest-value item in a recovery kit relative to their cost and weight.

## When a winch earns its place

A winch matters most for solo touring in terrain where a snatch recovery isn't possible — deep mud, steep embankments, or situations with no second vehicle available. For group touring on established tracks, a snatch strap and traction boards cover most scenarios.
